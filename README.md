# What it does:
One search, many publishers. sources.js hits OpenAlex, Crossref, and Europe PMC in parallel and merges the results — that spans Elsevier, Springer Nature, Wiley, IEEE, PLOS, MDPI, arXiv, bioRxiv and PubMed. Results are deduped by DOI and normalized title, then re-ranked on keyword fit, citations, recency, and whether multiple indexes agree. If one source is down the others still return; per-source counts show in the filter bar.
Easier to read. analyze.js splits the abstract into sentences, sorts them into What they wanted to know / How they did it / What they found / What it means, and swaps ~50 jargon patterns for plain English — every replacement keeps the original on hover. It also extracts study design, sample size, the number-bearing claims, and a "read this with caution" list, then scores how much weight the paper deserves. With an optional Anthropic key in Settings, a button re-does the rewrite with Claude.
Supports vs. argues against. The Evidence tab pools papers citing it, independent work on the same question, and same-topic meta-analyses, then labels each supports / challenges / related. Verified against real cases: on the power-posing paper it surfaces the Bayesian meta-analysis that undercuts it; on the gut-microbiome/obesity paper it finds both the confirming and the "overstated" critiques. Enabling Semantic Scholar in Settings adds the actual sentence a later paper used to cite this one, which is the most reliable signal available.
Scientists talking. The Voices tab resolves who actually publishes on the topic (OpenAlex author aggregation → affiliation, h-index, Wikipedia bio and portrait where the person really is a scientist), plus published interviews, news features, and editorials pulled from PubMed publication types.

# Two things worth knowing:
Interview discovery is deep-link based, not indexed. YouTube, podcast, and news APIs all need server-side keys, so a browser-only app can't pull those results inline. What it does instead is find the right people by name and hand you precise, pre-built searches into video, podcasts, and news for each of them. In-app interview results would need a small backend — say the word and I'll add one.
Stance labels are a reading aid, not a verdict. I tuned hard for precision after finding false positives (a constitutional-law book matching "power posing" on the word power; background sentences like "previous results have been contradictory" read as attacks). The gates I added cut those, at the cost of leaving more papers unlabeled. Confidence is shown on every call, and the evidence panel says so in the UI.
Non-biomedical topics work too — I tested ocean iron fertilization and it correctly named Philip Boyd and Andreas Oschlies as the leading voices, though journal commentary is thinner there since Europe PMC is biomedical.
